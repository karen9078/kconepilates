Studio Planning Guide

How to Choose a Pilates Equipment Mix for a New Studio

The equipment mix is a business decision, not just a shopping list. Choose the machines that match the classes you will teach, the clients you will serve and the revenue you need in the first two years.

Quick Answer

Anchor on reformers — they serve the widest class range and are the core revenue machine of a studio.

Add supporting equipment by class plan: a Cadillac for private and advanced work, a Wunda chair for small groups and versatility, and a ladder barrel or spine corrector for specific programming.

Match quantity to schedule, not to an idealized catalog — four to six reformers plus supporting pieces covers most single-room openings.

KCONE PILATES · 27 AUGUST 2026

Start with the class plan, not the catalog

A new studio often makes the same mistake: choosing equipment first and building classes around it. The more reliable order is the reverse — decide the class formats you will sell in the first twelve months, then choose the machines each format requires.

  • Mat-plus-equipment classes need reformers as the primary station.
  • Private sessions benefit from a Cadillac, which gives the teacher the widest exercise vocabulary with one client.
  • Small-group duet or trio classes are efficient on reformers and Wunda chairs.
  • Specialized programming — scoliosis, post-rehab, advanced articulation — uses barrels and spine correctors.

For the full launch sequence including room layout and budget, see How to Start a Pilates Studio: Equipment, Layout & Budget.

Why reformers anchor the mix

Reformers are the most flexible machine in the studio. They suit beginners through advanced clients, work in groups of almost any size, and generate the most billable hours per square meter. For most studios, reformers should form roughly 60-70% of the initial equipment budget.

If you are deciding between reformer grades or specifications, the professional Pilates reformer buying guide explains how to match a machine to studio use, daily load and expected traffic.

A practical starting mix

For a single-room studio opening with a core teaching team, a widely used starting configuration is:

  • Four to six reformers — the group-class backbone.
  • One Cadillac — private sessions, advanced work and teacher training.
  • One or two Wunda chairs — small groups, versatility and low space cost.
  • One ladder barrel or spine corrector — programming depth at a low investment.

This mix keeps capital controllable while giving teachers enough vocabulary to deliver a full schedule from day one.

Match the mix to your client profile

Different client profiles change the ideal mix:

  • Boutique group classes: lean heavily on reformers; keep one Cadillac for privates.
  • Rehabilitation and post-rehab: add a Cadillac and a tower, which give more supported, adjustable positions.
  • Teacher training: invest earlier in Cadillacs and barrels, since trainees need exposure to the full apparatus family.
  • Multi-discipline studio (Pilates + barre/yoga): reformers as the Pilates core, with a smaller supporting footprint.

Two machines to consider delaying

Not every apparatus needs to arrive on opening day. Two are common to defer:

  • A full second Cadillac — expensive per square meter; add it when private volume justifies it.
  • High-ticket specialty towers — valuable for training programs, but a half-tower or portable option often covers early needs at a fraction of the cost.

Planning for staged expansion keeps your first order realistic and gives you data on which classes actually sell before the second investment. This staged approach also works well for distributors building a sellable range; see How Pilates Equipment Distributors Can Evaluate a New Product Line.

Balance the mix against space and budget

Each machine competes for floor area and capital. A reformer needs roughly 2.5-3 m² of teaching space, a Cadillac more, and a chair much less. Frequently asked questions

How many reformers should a new Pilates studio start with?

Most single-room studios start with four to six reformers. Reformers are the core revenue machine because they serve the widest range of classes and clients, so they usually form 60-70% of the initial equipment budget.

What is a good first equipment mix for a new Pilates studio?

A practical starting mix is four to six reformers, one Cadillac, one Wunda chair and one ladder barrel or spine corrector. This covers mat-plus-equipment classes, private sessions and small groups while keeping the initial investment controllable.

Do I need a Cadillac on opening day?

One Cadillac is worth having from day one for private sessions and advanced work. A second Cadillac is usually better deferred until private and training volume justifies the space and capital.
