What Should Be Included in a Professional Pilates Equipment RFQ?

A well-structured request for quotation (RFQ) is the fastest way to get a comparable, accurate factory quote for professional Pilates equipment — and to avoid weeks of clarifying emails.

Quick Answer

An RFQ should state who you are and the project context: studio, distributor, chain buyer, or training facility, plus the intended use and expected opening timeline.

List the product categories and quantities you are evaluating — reformers, chairs, towers, Cadillacs, ladder barrels, spine correctors — rather than a single model name.

Add configuration and use details: expected daily load, professional or studio use, colors, branding, and any room or access constraints.

Ask for export packaging information and state your FOB port — KCONE quotes on FOB Shanghai or DDU — and request the documentation you need for approval.

Why a structured RFQ matters for professional equipment

Professional Pilates equipment is ordered by studios, distributors, chains and training facilities that compare multiple suppliers before committing. A structured RFQ gives every supplier the same baseline — categories, quantities, use scenario, packaging expectations and handover terms — so the quotations you receive are comparable.

When the same scope is sent to several factories, differences in price, lead time and packaging become visible instead of being hidden behind different assumptions. This is the difference between comparing quotations and comparing misunderstandings.

The core sections of a complete Pilates equipment RFQ

A complete RFQ can be written as one short document. It does not need every specification answered up front — it needs the fields that let a manufacturer return a meaningful quotation on the first reply.

1. Buyer profile and project context

  • Business type: studio, distributor, chain, training facility or other professional buyer.
  • Project context: a new studio, an expansion, a replacement order, or a distributor launch.
  • Target opening or delivery date, which sets the production schedule the quotation must fit.

2. Product categories and quantities

  • The categories under evaluation: reformers, Cadillacs, Wunda chairs, towers, ladder barrels, spine correctors and accessories.
  • Estimated quantity per category — even a range helps the factory plan material and production.
  • Whether this is a one-time order or a repeat/expansion program.

3. Use scenario and load expectations

  • Commercial studio use, training-facility use, or lower-frequency boutique use.
  • Expected sessions per day and typical user load, so the supplier confirms the right commercial grade.
  • Room dimensions, ceiling height and access constraints that affect equipment selection.

4. Configuration, colors and branding

  • Preferred frame colors and upholstery options.
  • Branding or logo requirements on frames, pads or packaging.
  • Any accessories under consideration: jump boards, boxes, extension straps or stands.

5. Packaging and documentation

  • Request export packaging information: method for frame and moving parts, protection for upholstery and springs, number of packages, packed dimensions and weight.
  • Request the documents you need for internal approval: specification sheets, product photos, videos and any certification documents relevant to your market.

6. Handover terms

  • Preferred trade terms: FOB Shanghai or DDU (for DDU, include the destination city and country).
  • Preferred payment method and any documentary requirements (invoice, packing list, certificate of origin).
  • Whether your own forwarder will handle the main international freight after port handover.

How a factory answers a complete RFQ

When an RFQ contains the fields above, a manufacturer can return a quotation that states the exact product category and configuration, quantity, included accessories, packaging scope, packed information, current production schedule, and the FOB Shanghai or DDU handover. Common RFQ mistakes to avoid

  • Sending only a model name without quantity, use scenario or packaging requirements — the quotation cannot be priced accurately.
  • Comparing quotations built on different assumptions — keep the same scope for every supplier.
  • Forgetting to state the FOB port — this changes logistics planning for the buyer's forwarder.
  • Not asking for packed dimensions and weight — these are needed for freight planning, not the product dimensions alone.

Frequently asked questions

What is a Pilates equipment RFQ?

A request for quotation (RFQ) is the document an overseas buyer sends to a manufacturer before ordering, listing business type, intended use, target categories, quantities, configuration requirements, packaging information, timeline and FOB port preference so the supplier can return an accurate quotation.

Should an RFQ name a specific product model?

It can, but categories with quantities and use context are more robust. Product lines are upgraded over time, so a category-based RFQ lets the factory quote the current configuration that fits your studio or distribution plan.

What FOB terms does KCONE quote on?

KCONE quotes on FOB Shanghai or DDU terms. The buyer's forwarder arranges the main international freight after port handover, so the preferred port should be stated in the first RFQ. Under DDU, KCONE arranges delivery to the buyer's destination and the buyer handles import duties and local clearance.
