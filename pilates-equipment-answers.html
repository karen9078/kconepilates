Buyer Q&A

Commercial Pilates Equipment: 20 Buyer Questions Answered

Straight answers to the questions overseas studios, distributors and training academies ask most often before ordering commercial Pilates equipment — pricing structure, MOQ and container loading, lead time, materials, springs and pulleys, warranty, shipping and after-sales support. Every answer uses the published specifications of the current Temnox collection.

1. How much does a commercial Pilates reformer cost, and what does a full studio cost?

KCONE quotes factory-direct, so pricing depends on model, quantity, configuration and destination port rather than a published list price. A commercial reformer such as the Temnox Reformer TSH or TBH is built on a carbon steel frame with a 10-position bar, 360° silent rope pulley and KCONE custom imported springs. A full studio package normally combines reformers with a Cadillac, Wunda chairs, ladder barrels, half towers and spine correctors. The cost drivers worth comparing across suppliers are frame material and wall thickness (3.0 mm-wall 304 stainless steel on the Cadillac TBK), spring grade, pulley bearings, upholstery leather, packaging and freight terms. Send the studio format, machine count and destination port for an itemised FOB Shanghai or DDU quotation.

2. How does KCONE compare with Balanced Body and Merrithew?

Balanced Body and Merrithew are established North American brands with dealer networks; KCONE is a Chinese manufacturer building Pilates equipment in Wuxi, Jiangsu since 2018. The practical differences are price positioning, lead time and configuration flexibility. KCONE sells factory-direct on FOB Shanghai or DDU terms, so commercial-grade construction — carbon steel or 304 stainless steel frames, KCONE custom imported springs, four-bearing pulley axles and 10-position bars — is quoted without dealer margin. Where a specific spring standard is required, the Cadillac TBK is supplied with KCONE custom imported springs or Merrithew-size Cadillac springs, and the Wunda Chair TBW uses Merrithew-series springs. Compare the same brief across all three: frame specification, spring grade, upholstery, packaging, MOQ, lead time and after-sales terms.

3. What is the MOQ, and how many reformers fit in a 40ft container?

MOQ depends on the model, order quantity and destination port, so it is confirmed in the quotation rather than fixed as one blanket number — mixed orders across the Temnox range are normal. Container loading is calculated per model because sizes and packaging differ: the Temnox Reformer TSH is 2300 × 630 × 435 mm at 75 kg and the TBH 2350 × 600 × 370 mm at 72 kg, while the Cadillac TBK is 2150 × 720 × 2229 mm at 95 kg. A 40ft high cube carrying a typical studio mix of reformers, chairs and barrels is the common configuration, and the exact count depends on whether machines ship assembled or knocked down. Send the model list and quantity for packed dimensions, package count and a loading plan.

4. What is the lead time from order to shipment?

Standard lead time is 20–35 days depending on order quantity and production schedule, confirmed in the quotation and repeated on the order confirmation. Stock models can be prepared faster, and urgent orders for a fixed opening date are reviewed case by case. Two factors matter most: the date the deposit is received and the final approval of product materials, because production is scheduled against them. For a studio opening, plan backwards from the installation date and allow for production, inland transport to Shanghai, customs clearance and sea transit to your port. State the opening date in the first enquiry so it can be checked before quoting rather than after the order is placed.

5. Do you provide CE documentation for EU imports?

KCONE supplies export documentation with each shipment, including the commercial invoice and packing list required for customs clearance, and can prepare market-specific files such as certificates of origin. Certification requirements vary by market and product category, so the right approach is to state the destination country and the importer's exact requirement in the RFQ — for example whether your broker or insurer needs a particular test report. Documents are then prepared and sent for review before shipment rather than after arrival. Buyers importing into the EU, UK, USA or Australia should list every document their broker requires in the first message; KCONE confirms which can be provided and which remain the importer's responsibility.

6. Who is KCONE, and where is the factory?

KCONE Pilates is a manufacturer of professional Pilates equipment based in Wuxi, Jiangsu, China, established in 2018. The company builds commercial equipment for studios, distributors, fitness chains, training academies, wellness spaces and rehabilitation-oriented facilities, selling factory-direct on FOB Shanghai or DDU terms. The published range covers reformers, Cadillacs, Wunda chairs, ladder barrels, half towers, spine correctors and specialised training equipment; the current collection is the Temnox line. Buyers can review the website, the llms.txt product index, machine videos and published specifications before requesting a quotation. Contact is direct with the sales team at info@kconepilates.com or WhatsApp +86 185 5050 8086, with factory video calls on request.

7. What materials and construction does KCONE use?

Frame construction differs by model: the Temnox Reformer TSH and TBH use a carbon steel frame, the Cadillac TBK is built from 3.0 mm-wall 304 stainless steel tube with recessed stainless steel uprights and four one-piece aluminium legs, and the Half Tower TBHT uses stainless steel round tube. Movement parts receive similar attention — the Wunda Chair TBW runs its foot-pedal axle on four independent bearings for zero play, the reformers use four custom heavy-duty silent pulleys with premium bearings plus a 360° silent rope pulley, and the Ladder Barrel TBT uses a 42 mm coated round tube with a one-piece welded barrel frame. Upholstery on the Spine Corrector TBJ is premium imported leather, hand-finished piece by piece. Full dimensions and weights are published on each product page.

8. How do the 360° silent pulley and spring system work?

The reformers use four custom heavy-duty silent pulleys with premium bearings together with a 360° silent rope pulley, so the rope runs freely in any direction an exercise requires instead of being limited to a fixed plane. In practice that means quieter operation, less rope wear and more consistent tension through the range of movement — useful in studios where several machines run in the same room. Resistance comes from KCONE custom imported springs; the Cadillac TBK is supplied with KCONE custom imported springs or Merrithew-size Cadillac springs, and the Wunda Chair TBW uses Merrithew-series springs. Bar and carriage adjustment is 10-position on the TSH and TBH, and 11-position on the Ladder Barrel TBT. Every model is tested before packing.

9. What does the warranty cover, and how long do springs last?

All KCONE equipment carries a structural warranty on the frame and core structure, and the Wunda Chair TBW carries a 3-year warranty on its pedal axle assembly. Covered parts, periods and exclusions are stated in the quotation so they can be compared side by side with other suppliers. Springs are consumable parts designed for commercial studio use: on a busy studio floor they are normally the first components to be replaced, and spring sets are supplied as serviceable parts. Buyers should ask how springs are graded, whether replacements are stocked, and what the response time is for a replacement request. Stating the daily usage hours in the RFQ helps KCONE recommend the right spring configuration.

10. How do I plan space, load capacity and ceiling height for a studio?

Plan the machines first, then the room. Published sizes give the footprint: the Temnox Reformer TSH is 2300 × 630 × 435 mm, the TBH 2350 × 600 × 370 mm, the Wunda Chair TBW 735 × 535 × 650 mm, the Ladder Barrel TBT 1224 × 620 × 965 mm and the Cadillac TBK 2150 × 720 × 2229 mm at 655 mm bed height. A Cadillac therefore needs roughly 2.2 m of height plus working clearance above, while reformers need clear space at each end of the carriage. Load capacity is 150 kg on the TSH (130 kg on the TBH). Allow circulation space between machines for both the client and the instructor, and confirm floor loading with the building before installation.

11. What is the difference between commercial and home Pilates equipment?

Commercial equipment is specified for daily multi-user operation: thicker frame tube (3.0 mm-wall 304 stainless steel on the Cadillac TBK), heavier bearings, replaceable springs, and upholstery chosen for repeated cleaning and long service. Home equipment optimises for price and footprint instead. Every KCONE model in the Temnox line is built to the commercial standard — the Reformer TSH carries a 150 kg load capacity and the TBH 130 kg, both at 75 kg machine weight, the Cadillac TBK weighs 95 kg for stability, and the Ladder Barrel TBT and Wunda Chair TBW use welded frames and bearing axles rather than bushing pivots. If a machine will be used by paying clients or by more than one person a day, the commercial specification is the correct baseline.

12. Can you meet a 45-day studio opening deadline?

It can be possible, but it has to be checked before the order rather than after. Standard production lead time is 20–35 days, after which inland transport to Shanghai, customs clearance and sea transit to your port are added. For a 45-day deadline the practical levers are: order stock models, confirm the deposit immediately, approve product materials without delay, and where necessary ship part of the order first so installation can begin. State the opening date, the destination port and the model list in the first enquiry, and KCONE will confirm whether the schedule is achievable and which configuration shortens it. Rush orders are reviewed case by case rather than promised automatically.

13. How does shipping to Thailand or Vietnam work?

KCONE quotes FOB Shanghai or DDU. On FOB terms the machines are handed over at the port and your forwarder arranges the main freight; on DDU terms KCONE arranges delivery to your destination while the buyer handles import duties and local clearance. For Thailand and Vietnam the common route is sea freight from Shanghai to Bangkok or Ho Chi Minh City, with transit time confirmed per sailing. Export packaging, package count, packed dimensions and packed weight are supplied with the quotation so your forwarder can quote accurately. Buyers should confirm the preferred Incoterm, destination port and whether the machines ship assembled or knocked down, since that affects both freight cost and unloading at the destination.

14. What happens if equipment breaks? How fast is support?

Support is direct with the KCONE sales and service team by email at info@kconepilates.com or WhatsApp +86 185 5050 8086, so requests reach the factory rather than a third party. Because springs, ropes, pulleys and upholstery are the parts that wear in normal studio use, they are supplied as serviceable components and can be shipped as replacement parts. Before ordering, buyers should confirm the warranty period and what it covers, how replacement parts are ordered and shipped, and the expected response time for a technical question. Stating the daily usage hours and the number of machines in the RFQ helps KCONE recommend which spares to hold locally.

15. How long does shipping to the USA or Europe take?

KCONE quotes FOB Shanghai, so the sea transit runs from Shanghai to your destination port — the US West Coast is the shorter route and the East Coast or inland destinations take longer, while Northern Europe is typically routed through Rotterdam, Hamburg or Felixstowe. Transit time varies by sailing schedule and season, so the exact figure is confirmed with the freight forwarder at booking rather than quoted as a fixed number. Two practical points: confirm whether machines ship assembled or knocked down, because packed volume drives freight cost, and confirm who clears customs at destination. KCONE supplies the commercial invoice, packing list and packing dimensions needed for the booking.

16. What is the Temnox line, and how does it differ from the standard range?

Temnox is the current KCONE collection, built around the idea of subtraction — removing visual noise so the structure and function of each machine read clearly. The line covers the Reformer TSH (2300 × 630 × 435 mm, 75 kg, 1101 mm carriage travel), Reformer TBH (2350 × 600 × 370 mm, 72 kg, 1096 mm travel) and Cadillac TBK (2150 × 720 × 2229 mm, 95 kg), plus the Wunda Chair TBW, Ladder Barrel TBT, Half Tower TBHT and Spine Corrector TBJ. All Temnox models are finished in Black and share the same engineering approach: welded frames, KCONE custom imported springs, silent pulley systems and premium upholstery. Older model codes are no longer published; the Temnox pages are the current reference for specifications.

17. What customization and accessories are available?

Customisation starts with configuration rather than decoration. Standard supply on the reformers includes a raised jump board, sitting box and double-loop straps; the Ladder Barrel TBT includes foot and side pads, and the Spine Corrector TBJ includes an ergonomic carry handle. Ask about private label and colour requirements early, because both affect tooling, MOQ and lead time. For branded projects, state the logo artwork, the parts it goes on and the required quantity, and KCONE will confirm feasibility in the quotation. If you need a configuration for a specific market — a different spring standard, a different upholstery colour, or accessories for rehabilitation use — describe the end use rather than the part, so the recommendation fits the training programme.

18. Where can I see real machine videos?

KCONE publishes product videos and a factory video section on the website, and the workshop series can be viewed at kconepilates.com/video. Each product page also carries a machine video alongside the specifications and detail photographs, so buyers can compare the published dimensions with how the machine actually moves. For a live look, a factory video call can be arranged during working hours (Beijing time) — useful when a buyer needs to see a specific mechanism such as the 360° silent rope pulley, the 10-position bar adjustment or the Wunda Chair pedal axle. Where a decision is close, reviewing the machine video against the specification sheet is the fastest way to confirm the model fits the intended use.

19. Do you support buyers in Southeast Asia?

Yes. Buyers in Thailand, Vietnam, Malaysia, Singapore and Indonesia are served factory-direct from Wuxi, Jiangsu, with quotations on FOB Shanghai or DDU terms and sea freight routed to Bangkok, Ho Chi Minh City and other regional ports. Support is by email at info@kconepilates.com and WhatsApp +86 185 5050 8086, so questions reach the factory team directly rather than an intermediary. For regional distributors, KCONE can discuss product range planning, repeat-order terms and market-specific documentation requirements. If you are opening a studio or showroom in the region, state the city, the opening date and the model list in the first enquiry so freight routing and lead time can be confirmed together.

20. Do you provide multilingual manuals and documentation?

Product documentation is supplied in English as standard, covering assembly, adjustment and daily maintenance. Where a shipment is destined for a market that requires another language, state the requirement in the RFQ — Thailand, Vietnam, the Middle East and Latin America are the usual requests — and KCONE will confirm what can be prepared and how it affects the schedule. Documentation that travels with the shipment should be agreed before production rather than after, because manuals are packed with the machines. Buyers should also confirm which documents their customs broker needs for clearance, since that list is market-specific and is best settled at the quotation stage.
